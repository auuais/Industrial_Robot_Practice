How to use these notes

Every chapter follows the slides of one lecture, then adds the explanations, pictures and practice that the slides leave out.

The course builds one idea on top of another. Lecture 2 gives you models (frames, kinematics, how to step a model forward in time). Lecture 3 makes the sensors trustworthy (calibration). Lecture 4 combines a model with a sensor to estimate a robot's position (odometry and the Kalman filter). Lecture 6 extends estimation to nonlinear models and to whole trajectories and maps. Lecture 7 is the optimization toolbox that sits underneath calibration, pose graphs and, later in the course, planning and control. Lecture 1 is the map that connects them, so start there; chapter S ties everything together and chapter G lists the Korean terms used on the slides.

Chapter numbers match the lecture numbers so you can jump between these notes and the slides (each chapter opens with a slide map). There was no Lecture 5 deck among the course files, so the numbering goes from 4 to 6. Numbers marked "recomputed" were re-derived for these notes and agree with the slides.

Practice questions

Each chapter ends with exam-style questions, each followed by a full answer. The tag says what the question tests:

The dots on the right show difficulty, from one dot (warm-up) to three (exam-hard). Cover the answer and try first — the act of retrieving is what makes it stick.

A three-pass plan
  1. Skim: for each chapter read the opening panel, the Key-idea boxes and the formula sheet at the end. You now know what the chapter is about.
  2. Work: read the sections in full and redo every worked example on paper without looking. Then attempt the questions, Concept ones first.
  3. Connect: finish with chapter S. Its mixed questions combine lectures the way exam questions tend to, and its formula sheet is a good last-day review.
